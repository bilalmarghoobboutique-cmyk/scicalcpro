Ohm's Law Explained with Examples: Complete Guide

📅 08 October 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read 👤 Bilal Marghoob
📢 Advertisement
Responsive — In-Content

Ohm's Law is the most fundamental formula in electrical engineering. It connects voltage, current, and resistance — three values that describe every electrical circuit. Whether you're studying physics, fixing a circuit, or designing electronics, Ohm's Law is your starting point. This guide explains it with clear examples.

1. What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it and inversely proportional to its resistance.

Discovered by German physicist Georg Simon Ohm in 1827, this law is the foundation of circuit analysis.

2. The Three Variables

  • Voltage (V) — Electrical pressure that pushes electrons. Measured in Volts (V). Symbol: V
  • Current (I) — Flow of electrons through a wire. Measured in Amperes (A). Symbol: I
  • Resistance (R) — Opposition to current flow. Measured in Ohms (Ω). Symbol: R

3. The Formula and Its Variations

The main formula:

V = I × R

Rearrange to find any value:

  • Voltage: V = I × R
  • Current: I = V / R
  • Resistance: R = V / I

4. The Ohm's Law Triangle

A handy memory trick: 

     ┌─────┐
     │  V  │
     ├──┬──┤
     │I │ R│
     └──┴──┘

Cover the value you want to find. If V is covered, you see I × R. If I is covered, you see V / R. If R is covered, you see V / I.

5. Step-by-Step Examples

Example 1: Find Voltage

Problem: A circuit has 2A current flowing through 6Ω resistance. What is the voltage?

  • Given: I = 2A, R = 6Ω
  • Formula: V = I × R
  • V = 2 × 6 = 12 Volts ✅

Example 2: Find Current

Problem: A 12V battery is connected to a 4Ω resistor. What's the current?

  • Given: V = 12V, R = 4Ω
  • Formula: I = V / R
  • I = 12 / 4 = 3 Amperes ✅

Example 3: Find Resistance

Problem: A 220V source drives 5A through a device. What is the resistance?

  • Given: V = 220V, I = 5A
  • Formula: R = V / I
  • R = 220 / 5 = 44 Ohms ✅

6. Power Formula (P = V × I)

Power is the rate at which electrical energy is used. Measured in Watts (W).

P = V × I

Example: A 220V device draws 5A. What is its power?

  • P = 220 × 5 = 1100 Watts (1.1 kW)

Other power formulas (using Ohm's Law):

  • P = I² × R
  • P = V² / R

7. Energy Formula (E = P × t)

Energy is the total electricity used over time. Measured in Watt-hours (Wh) or kilowatt-hours (kWh).

E = P × t

Example: A 1000W heater runs for 5 hours. How much energy does it use?

  • E = 1000 × 5 = 5000 Wh = 5 kWh

Real-world: Your electricity bill is based on kWh (units). 1 unit = 1 kWh.

8. Real-World Applications

  • Home wiring — Choosing wire gauge for a circuit
  • Electronics — Designing circuits, selecting resistors
  • Battery selection — Matching voltage and current needs
  • Motor design — Power and torque calculations
  • Solar panels — Calculating output voltage and current
  • Electric vehicles — Battery and motor calculations
