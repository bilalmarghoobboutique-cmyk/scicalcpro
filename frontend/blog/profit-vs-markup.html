Profit vs Markup: What's the Difference?

📅 08 October 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read 👤 Bilal Marghoob
📢 Advertisement
Responsive — In-Content

"I made 50% profit." But was that 50% margin or 50% markup? This is one of the most common confusions in business. Get it wrong, and you might price your products too low or too high. This guide explains the difference clearly with real examples.

  • 1. Definitions: Profit, Margin, Markup
  • 2. The Formulas
  • 3. Real Example

1. Definitions: Profit, Margin, Markup

Profit

The amount of money you earn after subtracting the cost from the selling price.

Formula: Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price

Profit Margin

Profit as a percentage of the selling price.

Formula: Margin % = (Profit / Selling Price) × 100

Markup

Profit as a percentage of the cost price.

Formula: Markup % = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100

2. The Formulas at a Glance

Profit = SP − CP
Margin % = ((SP − CP) / SP) × 100
Markup % = ((SP − CP) / CP) × 100

Where:

  • SP = Selling Price
  • CP = Cost Price

3. Real Example

Scenario: You buy a product for Rs 100 and sell it for Rs 150.

Step 1 — Profit:

  • Profit = 150 − 100 = Rs 50

Step 2 — Markup %:

  • Markup = (50 / 100) × 100 = 50%

Step 3 — Margin %:

  • Margin = (50 / 150) × 100 = 33.33%

So the same sale has:

  • Markup = 50%
  • Margin = 33.33%

4. Key Difference

The only difference is the denominator:

  • Margin uses Selling Price (SP) — the higher number
  • Markup uses Cost Price (CP) — the lower number

Because SP is usually higher than CP, margin is always lower than markup.

Relationship:

Markup % = Margin % / (1 − Margin % / 100)
Margin % = Markup % / (1 + Markup % / 100)

5. When to Use Each

Use Markup when:

  • You're pricing a product (adding profit to cost)
  • You're negotiating with suppliers
  • You want to know how much extra you're charging

Use Margin when:

  • You're analyzing business profitability
  • You're reporting to investors or accountants
  • You're comparing different products
  • You want to know what % of revenue is profit

6. Common Mistakes

  1. Confusing margin and markup — They are not the same. A 50% markup = 33.33% margin.
  2. Using markup for financial statements — Accountants use margin, not markup.
  3. Ignoring overhead costs — Real profit includes rent, salaries, etc.
  4. Pricing too low — If you target 50% margin but calculate using markup, you'll earn less than expected.
