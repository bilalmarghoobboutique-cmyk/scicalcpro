Area Formulas: Complete Guide with Examples for All Shapes

📅 08 October 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read 👤 Bilal Marghoob

Area is the amount of space inside a 2D shape. Whether you're tiling a floor, painting a wall, or solving a geometry problem, knowing the correct area formula is essential. This guide covers every common shape with formulas, examples, and real-world applications.

1. What is Area?

Area is measured in square units — square meters (m²), square feet (ft²), square centimeters (cm²), etc.

Think of area as: "How many 1×1 squares fit inside this shape?"

2. Square

A square has 4 equal sides and 4 right angles (90°).

Formula:

Area = side² = s × s
Perimeter = 4 × side

Example: Side = 5 cm

  • Area = 5² = 25 cm²
  • Perimeter = 4 × 5 = 20 cm

3. Rectangle

A rectangle has 4 right angles with opposite sides equal.

Formula:

Area = Length × Width = L × W
Perimeter = 2 × (L + W)

Example: Length = 10 m, Width = 6 m

  • Area = 10 × 6 = 60 m²
  • Perimeter = 2 × (10 + 6) = 32 m

4. Triangle

A triangle has 3 sides and 3 angles. There are multiple types (equilateral, isosceles, scalene, right-angled).

Formula (base × height):

Area = ½ × base × height
Perimeter = a + b + c (sum of all sides)

Example: Base = 10 cm, Height = 8 cm

  • Area = ½ × 10 × 8 = 40 cm²

Alternative formula (Heron's):

If you know all three sides a, b, c:

  • s = (a + b + c) / 2 (semi-perimeter)
  • Area = √(s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c))

5. Circle

A circle is a set of points equidistant from a center point.

Formula:

Area = π × r²
Circumference = 2 × π × r
Diameter = 2 × r

Example: Radius = 7 cm

  • Area = π × 7² = 3.14159 × 49 ≈ 153.94 cm²
  • Circumference = 2 × π × 7 ≈ 43.98 cm

If you know diameter (d): Area = π × (d/2)²

6. Parallelogram

A parallelogram has opposite sides parallel and equal.

Formula:

Area = base × height

Example: Base = 8 m, Height = 5 m

  • Area = 8 × 5 = 40 m²

7. Trapezoid (Trapezium)

A trapezoid has one pair of parallel sides (called bases).

Formula:

Area = ½ × (base1 + base2) × height

Example: base1 = 10, base2 = 6, height = 4

  • Area = ½ × (10 + 6) × 4 = ½ × 16 × 4 = 32

9. Real-World Applications

Area formulas are used every day:

  • Tiling a floor — Calculate floor area to know how many tiles to buy
  • Painting a wall — Wall area determines paint quantity
  • Gardening — Plot area for planting
  • Construction — Land area for property
  • Engineering — Cross-section area for stress calculations
  • Sports — Field area for games
